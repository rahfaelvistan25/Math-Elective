Part 1

Introduction to Business Functions

1. What is a Function?

A function is a mathematical rule that tells us how one value changes when another value changes.

In business, we use functions to calculate things such as:

  • How much it costs to produce products.
  • How much money a business earns from selling products.
  • How much profit a business makes.
  • How many products customers want to buy.
  • How many products businesses are willing to sell.
Simple Example

Selling Bottled Water

Imagine you are selling bottled water.

  • Selling price: ₱20 per bottle
  • Number of bottles sold: 10

To calculate your total sales:

\[ ₱20 \times 10 = ₱200 \]

Your total sales are ₱200.

What happens when you sell 20 bottles?

\[ ₱20 \times 20 = ₱400 \]

The more bottles you sell, the higher your total sales.

This relationship can be written as:

\[ \mathbf{R(x) = 20x} \]

Where:

  • \(R(x)\) = Total revenue or sales
  • \(20\) = Selling price per bottle
  • \(x\) = Number of bottles sold
Remember:

The letter x usually represents the number of products.

The letter R represents revenue, C represents cost, and P represents profit.

Part 2

Linear Cost Function

2. What is Cost?

Cost is the total amount of money a business spends to operate or produce products.

There are two basic types of cost:

A. Fixed Cost

Fixed cost is an expense that stays the same even when the business produces more or fewer products.

Examples:

  • Monthly rent: ₱5,000
  • Internet bill: ₱1,000
  • Monthly equipment rental: ₱2,000
Even when you produce zero products, you may still need to pay rent.

B. Variable Cost

Variable cost changes depending on how many products you produce.

Examples:

  • Materials for each product: ₱20
  • Packaging for each product: ₱5
  • Ingredient cost for each milk tea: ₱30
When you produce more products, the total variable cost increases.

Formula: Linear Cost Function

\[ \mathbf{C(x) = F + vx} \]
\(C(x)\) = Total cost • \(F\) = Fixed cost • \(v\) = Variable cost for each product • \(x\) = Number of products

How to identify the given numbers in a word problem

Clue in the problem What it means
Monthly rent is ₱5,000Fixed cost (\(F\))
Materials cost ₱20 eachVariable cost (\(v\))
Produce 100 notebooksQuantity (\(x\))
Find the total expenseTotal cost \(C(x)\)
Example 1

Find the Total Cost

Problem: A small business produces notebooks.

  • Monthly rent: ₱3,000
  • Materials for each notebook: ₱20
  • Number of notebooks produced: 100

How much is the total cost?

Step 1
Identify the given values: \[ F = ₱3,000,\quad v = ₱20,\quad x = 100 \]
Step 2
Write the formula: \[ C(x) = F + vx \]
Step 3
Substitute the numbers: \[ C(100) = 3,000 + 20(100) \]
Step 4
Multiply first: \[ 20 \times 100 = 2,000 \] \[ C(100) = 3,000 + 2,000 \]
Step 5
Add: \[ C(100) = 5,000 \]
Final Answer:

₱5,000 total cost

Explanation: The business spends ₱3,000 on rent and ₱2,000 on materials. Therefore, the total cost is ₱5,000.

Example 2

Find the Number of Products

Problem: A business spends ₱8,000 in total. Its fixed cost is ₱3,000, and each product costs ₱25 to produce.

How many products did the business produce?

Step 1
Identify the given values:
Total cost = ₱8,000
Fixed cost = ₱3,000
Variable cost = ₱25
Quantity (\(x\)) = ?
Step 2
Write the formula: \[ C(x) = F + vx \]
Step 3
Substitute the values: \[ 8,000 = 3,000 + 25x \]
Step 4
Move the fixed cost to the other side: \[ 8,000 - 3,000 = 25x \] \[ 5,000 = 25x \]
Step 5
Divide by 25: \[ x = 5,000 \div 25 \] \[ x = 200 \]
Final Answer:

200 products

Explanation: The business produced 200 products because its total variable cost was ₱5,000, and each product costs ₱25.

Part 3

Nonlinear Cost Function

3. What is a Nonlinear Cost Function?

A nonlinear cost function is a function where the cost does not increase at a constant rate.

In a linear function, the cost increases by the same amount for every additional product. In a nonlinear function, the increase may become larger or smaller as production increases.

For example:
  • Producing a small number of products may be inexpensive.
  • Producing too many products may require additional machines, electricity, overtime, or maintenance.
  • These additional expenses can cause the cost to increase faster.

Common Formula

\[ \mathbf{C(x) = ax^2 + bx + F} \]
\(C(x)\) = Total cost • \(a\) and \(b\) = Numbers given in the problem • \(x\) = Number of products • \(F\) = Fixed cost • \(x^2\) = \(x\) multiplied by itself
Remember:

If you see x², it means: \(x^2 = x \times x\)

For example: \(5^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25\), and \(10^2 = 10 \times 10 = 100\).

Example

Calculate the Nonlinear Cost

Problem: A business has the following cost function:

\[ C(x) = x^2 + 10x + 1,000 \]

Find the total cost when the business produces 10 products.

Step 1
Identify the given values: \[ x = 10 \] \[ C(x) = x^2 + 10x + 1,000 \]
Step 2
Replace every \(x\) with 10: \[ C(10) = 10^2 + 10(10) + 1,000 \]
Step 3
Calculate the exponent: \[ 10^2 = 100 \] \[ C(10) = 100 + 100 + 1,000 \]
Step 4
Add the numbers: \[ 100 + 100 = 200 \] \[ 200 + 1,000 = 1,200 \]
Final Answer:

₱1,200

Explanation: When the business produces 10 products, its total cost is ₱1,200.

Part 4

Revenue Function

4. What is Revenue?

Revenue is the total amount of money a business receives from selling products or services.

In simple terms: Revenue = Selling Price × Number of Products Sold

Formula

\[ \mathbf{R(x) = px} \]
\(R(x)\) = Total revenue • \(p\) = Selling price of each product • \(x\) = Number of products sold

How to identify the given numbers

Clue in the problem What it means
Each shirt costs ₱250Selling price (\(p\))
Sold 20 shirtsQuantity sold (\(x\))
Find total salesRevenue \(R(x)\)
Example 1

Find the Revenue

Problem: A store sells bags for ₱500 each. The store sells 20 bags.

How much is its total revenue?

Step 1
Identify the given values: Selling price = ₱500, Quantity sold = 20
Step 2
Write the formula: \(R(x) = px\)
Step 3
Substitute the values: \(R(20) = 500 \times 20\)
Step 4
Multiply: \(R(20) = 10,000\)
Final Answer:

₱10,000 revenue

Explanation: The store receives ₱10,000 from selling 20 bags at ₱500 each.

Example 2

Find the Number of Products Sold

Problem: A store earns ₱15,000 from selling products at ₱300 each.

How many products were sold?

Step 1
Identify values: Revenue = ₱15,000, Selling price = ₱300, Quantity = ?
Step 2
Write the formula: \(R(x) = px\)
Step 3
Substitute: \(15,000 = 300x\)
Step 4
Divide by 300: \(x = 15,000 \div 300 = 50\)
Final Answer:

50 products

Example 3

When the Selling Price Changes

Sometimes, the selling price changes depending on the number of products sold.

Problem: A business sells products according to this price function: \[ p(x) = 100 - 2x \]

Find its total revenue when it sells 10 products.

Step 1
Identify values: \(x = 10\), Price function \(p(x) = 100 - 2x\)
Step 2
Find the selling price: Replace \(x\) with 10: \[ p(10) = 100 - 2(10) = 100 - 20 = 80 \] The selling price is ₱80 per product.
Step 3
Calculate the revenue: Revenue = Price × Quantity \[ R(10) = 80 \times 10 = 800 \]
Final Answer:

₱800 revenue

Important: When the selling price changes with quantity, find the price first before calculating revenue.
Part 5

Profit Function

5. What is Profit?

Profit is the money remaining after the business pays all its costs.

In simple terms: Profit = Revenue - Cost

There are three possible results:

Positive profit

The business earns money.

Zero profit

The business earns exactly enough to cover its costs (Breaks even).

Negative profit

The business loses money.

Formula

\[ \mathbf{P(x) = R(x) - C(x)} \]
\(P(x)\) = Profit • \(R(x)\) = Revenue • \(C(x)\) = Total cost
Example 1

Calculate the Profit

Problem: A keyboard shop sells keyboards for ₱150 each. The business has:

  • Fixed cost: ₱2,000
  • Production cost per keyboard: ₱50
  • Number of keyboards sold: 40

Find the profit.

Step 1
Identify values: Selling price = ₱150, Fixed cost = ₱2,000, Variable cost = ₱50, Quantity = 40
Step 2
Calculate the revenue: \[ R(x) = \text{Selling Price} \times \text{Quantity} \] \[ R(40) = 150 \times 40 = 6,000 \implies \text{Revenue} = ₱6,000 \]
Step 3
Calculate the total cost: \[ C(x) = F + vx \] \[ C(40) = 2,000 + 50(40) \] \[ 50 \times 40 = 2,000 \] \[ C(40) = 2,000 + 2,000 = 4,000 \implies \text{Total cost} = ₱4,000 \]
Step 4
Calculate the profit: \[ P(x) = R(x) - C(x) \] \[ P(40) = 6,000 - 4,000 = 2,000 \]
Final Answer:

₱2,000 profit

Explanation: The shop earns ₱6,000 from sales and spends ₱4,000 on expenses. The remaining ₱2,000 is its profit.

Example 2

Determine Whether the Business Earns or Loses Money

Problem: A store earns ₱5,000 in revenue. Its total expenses are ₱7,000.

Find its profit or loss.

Step 1
Identify values: Revenue = ₱5,000, Cost = ₱7,000
Step 2
Write formula: Profit = Revenue - Cost
Step 3
Substitute: Profit = 5,000 - 7,000
Step 4
Subtract: Profit = -2,000
Final Answer:

₱2,000 LOSS

Why is it a loss?

Because the business spent ₱7,000 but earned only ₱5,000.

  • If revenue is greater than cost, the business has a profit.
  • If revenue is smaller than cost, the business has a loss.
  • If revenue equals cost, the business breaks even.
Part 6

Demand Function

6. What is Demand?

Demand refers to how many products customers are willing and able to buy at a certain price.

The Law of Demand

When the price goes up, customers usually buy fewer products.

When the price goes down, customers usually buy more products.

Formula

\[ \mathbf{Q_d = a - bp} \]
\(Q_d\) = Quantity demanded • \(a\) = Starting quantity • \(b\) = Number showing how much demand changes when price changes • \(p\) = Price of the product

For beginner exercises, the values of \(a\) and \(b\) will usually be given.

Example 1

Calculate the Quantity Demanded

Problem: A store estimates its customer demand using this formula: \[ Q_d = 120 - 2p \]

How many products will customers buy if the selling price is ₱20?

Step 1
Identify values: Price (\(p\)) = 20, Demand formula = \(120 - 2p\)
Step 2
Substitute price: \(Q_d = 120 - 2(20)\)
Step 3
Multiply: \(2 \times 20 = 40 \implies Q_d = 120 - 40\)
Step 4
Subtract: \(Q_d = 80\)
Final Answer:

80 products

Explanation: When the price is ₱20, the estimated quantity demanded is 80 products.

Example 2

Find the Price

Problem: The demand function is: \[ Q_d = 120 - 2p \]

If customers want to buy 70 products, what is the price?

Step 1
Identify values: Quantity demanded = 70, Formula = \(120 - 2p\)
Step 2
Replace \(Q_d\) with 70: \(70 = 120 - 2p\)
Step 3
Subtract 120 from both sides: \(70 - 120 = -2p \implies -50 = -2p\)
Step 4
Divide both sides by -2: \(p = -50 \div -2 = 25\)
Final Answer:

₱25

Explanation: If customers want to buy 70 products, the estimated price is ₱25.

Part 7

Supply Function

7. What is Supply?

Supply refers to how many products businesses are willing and able to sell at a certain price.

The Law of Supply

When the selling price increases, businesses usually want to produce and sell more products.

When the selling price decreases, businesses usually want to produce and sell fewer products.

Formula

\[ \mathbf{Q_s = c + dp} \]
\(Q_s\) = Quantity supplied • \(c\) = Starting quantity • \(d\) = Number showing how much supply changes when price changes • \(p\) = Price
Example 1

Calculate the Quantity Supplied

Problem: A business uses the following supply function: \[ Q_s = 10 + 3p \]

How many products will the business supply if the price is ₱20?

Step 1
Identify values: Price = ₱20, Supply formula = \(10 + 3p\)
Step 2
Substitute the price: \(Q_s = 10 + 3(20)\)
Step 3
Multiply: \(3 \times 20 = 60 \implies Q_s = 10 + 60\)
Step 4
Add: \(Q_s = 70\)
Final Answer:

70 products

Example 2

Find the Price

Problem: The supply function is: \[ Q_s = 10 + 3p \]

If the business supplies 100 products, what is the price?

Step 1
Replace \(Q_s\) with 100: \(100 = 10 + 3p\)
Step 2
Subtract 10 from both sides: \(100 - 10 = 3p \implies 90 = 3p\)
Step 3
Divide by 3: \(p = 90 \div 3 = 30\)
Final Answer:

₱30

Part 8

Market Equilibrium

8. What is Market Equilibrium?

Market equilibrium happens when the quantity customers want to buy is equal to the quantity businesses want to sell.

In simple terms: Demand = Supply

At this point, the market has an equilibrium price and an equilibrium quantity.

Formula

\[ \mathbf{Q_d = Q_s} \]
Example

Find the Equilibrium Price and Quantity

Problem: A market has the following functions:

  • Demand: \[ Q_d = 120 - 2p \]
  • Supply: \[ Q_s = 10 + 3p \]

Find the equilibrium price and quantity.

Step 1
Make demand equal to supply: \[ Q_d = Q_s \] \[ 120 - 2p = 10 + 3p \]
Step 2
Move numbers to the left side and price terms to the right side: \[ 120 - 10 = 3p + 2p \]
Step 3
Subtract and combine: \[ 110 = 5p \]
Step 4
Divide by 5: \[ p = 110 \div 5 = 22 \] \[ \mathbf{\text{Equilibrium Price} = ₱22} \]
Step 5
Find the equilibrium quantity: Use either the demand or supply formula. Let's use demand: \[ Q_d = 120 - 2p \] Replace \(p\) with 22: \[ Q_d = 120 - 2(22) \] Multiply: \[ Q_d = 120 - 44 = 76 \] \[ \mathbf{\text{Equilibrium Quantity} = 76\text{ products}} \]
Final Answers:

• Equilibrium price = ₱22

• Equilibrium quantity = 76 products

Remember: First, make demand equal to supply. Solve for the price. Then substitute the price into either formula to find the quantity.
Part 9

Break-Even Analysis

9. What is Break-Even Analysis?

Break-even analysis helps a business determine how many products it must sell before it stops losing money.

At the break-even point: Total Revenue = Total Cost

This means the business is not earning a profit, but it is also not experiencing a loss (\(\text{Profit} = 0\)).

Important Terms

TermMeaning
Fixed cost (\(F\))Expenses that do not change with the number of products.
Variable cost (\(v\))Cost for producing one product.
Selling price (\(p\))Amount received for each product.
Break-even quantity (\(Q\))Number of products that must be sold to cover all expenses.

Formula

Break-even Quantity = Fixed Cost ÷ (Selling Price - Variable Cost per Product)

\[ \mathbf{Q = \frac{F}{p - v}} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{Q = F \div (p - v)} \]
\(Q\) = Break-even quantity • \(F\) = Fixed cost • \(p\) = Selling price per product • \(v\) = Variable cost per product
Example

Find the Break-Even Quantity (Milk Tea Business)

Problem: A milk tea business has the following expenses:

  • Monthly rent: ₱3,000
  • Cost to make one milk tea: ₱50
  • Selling price of one milk tea: ₱80

How many milk teas must the business sell to break even?

Step 1
Identify values: Fixed cost (\(F\)) = ₱3,000, Selling price (\(p\)) = ₱80, Variable cost (\(v\)) = ₱50, Break-even quantity = ?
Step 2
Write formula: \(Q = F \div (p - v)\)
Step 3
Substitute values: \(Q = 3,000 \div (80 - 50)\)
Step 4
Subtract inside parentheses first: \(80 - 50 = 30 \implies Q = 3,000 \div 30\)
Step 5
Divide: \(Q = 100\)
Final Answer:

100 milk teas

Explanation: The business must sell 100 milk teas to recover its fixed and variable costs.

Understanding Profit and Loss Around the Break-Even Point

For the same milk tea business (\(F = ₱3,000\), \(p = ₱80\), \(v = ₱50\)):

Products sold Revenue Total cost Result
50₱4,000₱5,500₱1,500 loss
100₱8,000₱8,000Break-even (₱0)
120₱9,600₱9,000₱600 profit
Why does this happen?

At 50 products:
Revenue = \(50 \times 80 = ₱4,000\)
Cost = \(3,000 + (50 \times 50) = 3,000 + 2,500 = ₱5,500\)
Profit = \(4,000 - 5,500 = -₱1,500\) (The business loses ₱1,500).

At 100 products:
Revenue = \(100 \times 80 = ₱8,000\)
Cost = \(3,000 + (100 \times 50) = 3,000 + 5,000 = ₱8,000\)
Profit = \(8,000 - 8,000 = ₱0\) (The business breaks even).

At 120 products:
Revenue = \(120 \times 80 = ₱9,600\)
Cost = \(3,000 + (120 \times 50) = 3,000 + 6,000 = ₱9,000\)
Profit = \(9,600 - 9,000 = ₱600\) (The business earns ₱600).

Important: The selling price must be higher than the variable cost for the business to recover its fixed costs through sales. If the selling price is equal to or lower than the variable cost, there is no positive break-even quantity under this simple model.

Part 10

Introduction to Limits

10. What is a Limit?

A limit is a mathematical idea that tells us what value a function is approaching when \(x\) gets closer to a particular number.

In simpler words: A limit asks: What number does the answer get closer to?

Example: Understanding the Idea

Imagine these numbers for the function: \[ f(x) = 2x + 3 \]

\(x\) \(2x + 3\) Observation
15Starting point
27Increasing
39Getting closer to 11
411Target value is 11

When \(x\) approaches 4, the answer approaches 11. We write this as:

\[ \mathbf{\lim_{x\to 4} (2x + 3) = 11} \]
Read this as: "The limit of 2x plus 3, as x approaches 4, is 11."

What Does "x Approaches 4" Mean?

It means \(x\) gets closer to 4.

3.9 is close to 4 3.99 is closer to 4 3.999 is even closer to 4

We want to know what answer the function approaches.

Part 11

Evaluating Limits by Direct Substitution

11. The Easiest Way to Solve Limits

When a function is simple, we can find its limit by directly replacing \(x\) with the number given. This is called direct substitution.

Steps:

  1. Look at the function.
  2. Find the number that \(x\) approaches.
  3. Replace every \(x\) with that number.
  4. Calculate the answer.
Example 1

Solve a Simple Limit

Problem: Evaluate: \[ \lim_{x\to 3} (2x + 4) \]

Step 1
Identify the number: \(x\) approaches 3.
Step 2
Replace \(x\) with 3: \(2(3) + 4\)
Step 3
Multiply: \(2 \times 3 = 6 \implies 6 + 4\)
Step 4
Add: \(6 + 4 = 10\)
Final Answer:

10

Example 2

Solve Another Limit

Problem: Evaluate: \[ \lim_{x\to 5} (3x + 2) \]

Step 1
Replace \(x\) with 5: \(3(5) + 2\)
Step 2
Multiply: \(3 \times 5 = 15 \implies 15 + 2\)
Step 3
Add: \(15 + 2 = 17\)
Final Answer:

17

Example 3

Limit with Squared Numbers

Problem: Evaluate: \[ \lim_{x\to 4} (x^2 + 2x) \]

Step 1
Replace \(x\) with 4: \(4^2 + 2(4)\)
Step 2
Calculate the square: \(4^2 = 4 \times 4 = 16 \implies 16 + 8\)
Step 3
Add: \(16 + 8 = 24\)
Final Answer:

24

Remember: For direct substitution, simply replace \(x\) with the number beside the arrow.
Part 12

Limits that Result in Zero Divided by Zero

12. What Happens When Direct Substitution Gives 0/0?

Sometimes, replacing \(x\) with the given number produces: \[ 0 \div 0 \] This is called an indeterminate form.

It does not mean the answer is automatically zero. It means we need to simplify the expression first. One beginner-friendly way is factoring.

What is Factoring?

Factoring means changing an expression into multiplication form.

For example: \[ x^2 - 9 \]

We can write it as: \[ (x - 3)(x + 3) \]

This is because: \((x - 3)(x + 3) = x^2 - 9\).

Example

Solve a Limit by Factoring

Problem: Find: \[ \lim_{x\to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} \]

Step 1
Try direct substitution: Replace \(x\) with 3: \[ \frac{3^2 - 9}{3 - 3} \] Calculate top: \(9 - 9 = 0\). Calculate bottom: \(3 - 3 = 0\).
We get 0/0. We cannot finish using direct substitution.
Step 2
Factor the numerator: \[ x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3) \] Our expression becomes: \[ \lim_{x\to 3} \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{x - 3} \]
Step 3
Cancel the common factor: The \((x - 3)\) on the top and bottom can be cancelled for \(x\) values near 3 but not equal to 3.
We are left with: \[ x + 3 \]
Step 4
Replace \(x\) with 3: \[ 3 + 3 = 6 \]
Final Answer:

6

Important Reminder When Solving Limits:
  • Try direct substitution first.
  • If you get an ordinary number, that is usually your answer.
  • If you get 0/0, simplify the expression (often by factoring).
  • Substitute again after simplifying.
Part 13

One-Sided Limits

13. What are One-Sided Limits?

Sometimes a function behaves differently depending on which side we approach from. There are two types:

A. Left-Hand Limit

We approach the number from values smaller than the given number.

\[ \lim_{x\to a^-} f(x) \]
The small minus sign means approach from the left.

B. Right-Hand Limit

We approach the number from values greater than the given number.

\[ \lim_{x\to a^+} f(x) \]
The small plus sign means approach from the right.
Example

Delivery Fee Rates

A delivery company uses these rates:

  • Delivery fee is ₱50 for distances below 5 km.
  • Delivery fee is ₱80 for distances of 5 km or more.

The function is:

\[ D(x) = \begin{cases} 50, & x < 5 \\ 80, & x \geq 5 \end{cases} \]

What happens when the delivery distance approaches 5 km?

Step 1
Approach from the left: From the left, distance is less than 5 km. The delivery fee is ₱50.
Left-hand limit = ₱50.
Step 2
Approach from the right: From the right, distance is greater than 5 km. The delivery fee is ₱80.
Right-hand limit = ₱80.
Step 3
Compare the answers: Left-hand limit (₱50) \(\ne\) Right-hand limit (₱80). They are not equal.
Final Answer:

The two-sided limit does not exist.

Why? Because the two sides approach different values.

Remember: A two-sided limit exists only when the left-hand and right-hand limits are equal.
Part 14

Continuity in Business Functions

14. What is Continuity?

Continuity means a function has no break, jump, or hole at a particular point.

In simple terms: A continuous function has a connected graph at the point being examined.

Examples of everyday continuous relationships include:

  • A taxi fare that increases smoothly with distance.
  • A regular hourly wage calculation based on hours worked.
  • A simple production cost function where every additional unit adds a fixed cost.

Three Conditions for Continuity

For a function to be continuous at \(x = a\), three conditions must be satisfied:

Condition 1

Defined at that point

There must be an actual value for \(f(a)\).

Condition 2

The limit must exist

The left-hand and right-hand limits must be equal.

Condition 3

Limit equals value

\[ \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a) \]

If all three conditions are satisfied, the function is continuous at that point.

Example 1

Continuous Function

Given: \[ f(x) = 2x + 4 \]

Check whether the function is continuous at \(x = 3\).

Step 1
Find function value: \(f(3) = 2(3) + 4 = 6 + 4 = 10\)
Step 2
Find the limit: \(\lim_{x\to 3} (2x + 4) = 2(3) + 4 = 10\)
Step 3
Compare results: Function value = 10, Limit = 10. They are equal!
Final Answer:

The function is continuous at \(x = 3\).

Example 2

Discontinuous Function

Consider the delivery fee:

\[ D(x) = \begin{cases} 50, & x < 5 \\ 80, & x \geq 5 \end{cases} \]

Check the function at \(x = 5\).

Step 1
Find function value: Since \(x = 5\) belongs to second rule: \(D(5) = 80\)
Step 2
Find left-hand limit: From left (\(x<5\)), fee is ₱50. Left-hand limit = 50.
Step 3
Find right-hand limit: From right (\(x\geq 5\)), fee is ₱80. Right-hand limit = 80.
Step 4
Compare the two limits: 50 is not equal to 80. The two-sided limit does not exist.
Final Answer:

The function is discontinuous at \(x = 5\).

Part 16

How to Solve Business Word Problems

Whenever students encounter a word problem, follow these five steps:

1
STEP 1: Read the problem carefully.

Understand what the question is asking.

2
STEP 2: Find the given numbers.

Look for the following clues:

Rent or monthly expenses = Fixed cost (\(F\)) Cost per item = Variable cost (\(v\)) Selling price = Price (\(p\)) Number of products = Quantity (\(x\)) Total sales = Revenue (\(R(x)\)) Total expenses = Total cost (\(C(x)\))
3
STEP 3: Choose the correct formula.

For example:

  • If the question asks for total cost, use: C(x) = F + vx
  • If the question asks for profit, use: P(x) = R(x) - C(x)
4
STEP 4: Substitute the given values.

Replace the letters with the actual numbers from the problem.

5
STEP 5: Calculate and write the final answer.

Show the multiplication, addition, subtraction, or division. Include the correct unit, such as pesos or products.

Student Practice Exercises

Test Your Understanding (16 Beginner Problems)

A. Linear Cost Function

Problem 1

Monthly Rent & Production Cost

1. A business has a monthly rent of ₱2,000. Each product costs ₱30 to produce. Find the total cost of producing 100 products.

Solution 1

Given: \(F = 2,000,\quad v = 30,\quad x = 100\)

Formula: \(C(x) = F + vx\)

Substitute: \(C(100) = 2,000 + 30(100)\)

Multiply: \(30 \times 100 = 3,000\)

Add: \(2,000 + 3,000 = 5,000\)

Answer: ₱5,000

Problem 2

Finding Quantity from Total Cost

2. A business spends ₱6,000 in total. Its fixed cost is ₱2,000, and each product costs ₱20 to produce. How many products were produced?

Solution 2

Given: \(C(x) = 6,000,\quad F = 2,000,\quad v = 20\)

Formula: \(C(x) = F + vx\)

Substitute: \(6,000 = 2,000 + 20x\)

Subtract: \(6,000 - 2,000 = 20x \implies 4,000 = 20x\)

Divide: \(x = 4,000 \div 20\)

Answer: 200 products

B. Nonlinear Cost Function

Problem 3

Quadratic Cost Calculation

3. Given the cost function: \[ C(x) = x^2 + 5x + 500 \]

Find the total cost when \(x = 10\).

Solution 3

Given: \(C(x) = x^2 + 5x + 500,\quad x = 10\)

Substitute: \(C(10) = 10^2 + 5(10) + 500\)

Calculate square: \(10^2 = 100\)

Multiply: \(5 \times 10 = 50\)

Add: \(100 + 50 + 500 = 650\)

Answer: ₱650

C. Revenue Function

Problem 4

Shirt Sales Revenue

4. A store sells shirts for ₱250 each. It sells 40 shirts. Find the total revenue.

Solution 4

\(R(x) = px\)

\(R(40) = 250 \times 40\)

Answer: ₱10,000

Problem 5

Finding Quantity Sold from Revenue

5. A store earns ₱12,000 by selling products at ₱300 each. How many products did it sell?

Solution 5

\(R(x) = px\)

\(12,000 = 300x\)

\(x = 12,000 \div 300\)

Answer: 40 products

D. Profit Function

Problem 6

Finding Profit from Revenue and Cost

6. A business sells each product for ₱100. Its fixed cost is ₱1,000, and its variable cost is ₱40 per product. Find the profit when it sells 30 products.

Solution 6

Given: Selling price = ₱100, Fixed cost = ₱1,000, Variable cost = ₱40, Quantity = 30

First, find revenue: \(R(30) = 100 \times 30 = 3,000\)

Next, find cost: \(C(30) = 1,000 + 40(30) = 1,000 + 1,200 = 2,200\)

Finally, find profit: \(P(30) = 3,000 - 2,200\)

Answer: ₱800 profit

E. Demand Function

Problem 7

Quantity Demanded at ₱20

7. Given: \[ Q_d = 100 - 2p \]

Find the quantity demanded when the price is ₱20.

Solution 7

\(Q_d = 100 - 2p,\quad p = 20\)

\(Q_d = 100 - 2(20) = 100 - 40\)

Answer: 60 products

Problem 8

Finding Price for 60 Units Demanded

8. Using the same demand function, find the price when the quantity demanded is 60.

Solution 8

\(Q_d = 100 - 2p\)

\(60 = 100 - 2p\)

\(60 - 100 = -2p \implies -40 = -2p\)

\(p = -40 \div -2\)

Answer: ₱20

F. Supply Function

Problem 9

Quantity Supplied at ₱20

9. Given: \[ Q_s = 10 + 2p \]

Find the quantity supplied when the price is ₱20.

Solution 9

\(Q_s = 10 + 2p,\quad p = 20\)

\(Q_s = 10 + 2(20) = 10 + 40\)

Answer: 50 products

G. Break-Even Analysis

Problem 10

Break-Even Quantity

10. A business has a fixed cost of ₱4,000. Each product sells for ₱100, and the variable cost is ₱60 per product. How many products must be sold to break even?

Solution 10

\(Q = F \div (p - v)\)

\(Q = 4,000 \div (100 - 60)\)

\(Q = 4,000 \div 40\)

Answer: 100 products

Final Summary

Final Summary for Students

Remember these simple ideas:

1

Cost

Money a business spends to operate or produce.

2

Revenue

Money a business receives from sales.

3

Profit

Revenue minus total cost.

4

Demand

How many products customers want to buy.

5

Supply

How many products businesses want to sell.

6

Equilibrium

Happens when demand equals supply (\(Q_d = Q_s\)).

7

Break-Even

Happens when total revenue equals total cost.

8

Limit

Tells us what value a function approaches.

9

Continuity

Means a function has no break at the point being examined.

The Most Important Formulas to Memorize:

  • • Total Cost = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost × Quantity \(\quad [C(x) = F + vx]\)
  • • Revenue = Selling Price × Quantity \(\quad [R(x) = px]\)
  • • Profit = Revenue - Total Cost \(\quad [P(x) = R(x) - C(x)]\)
  • • Break-Even Quantity = Fixed Cost ÷ (Selling Price - Variable Cost) \(\quad [Q = \frac{F}{p - v}]\)
  • • Equilibrium = Demand = Supply \(\quad [Q_d = Q_s]\)
Study Tip:

Do not memorize the formulas alone. Practice identifying the given numbers in the problem first. Once you know what each number represents, choosing the correct formula becomes much easier.

The next useful classroom activity is a short guided worksheet where students identify the given values first, choose the formula, and then solve each problem independently.

Step-by-Step Problem Solving • Core Formulas • Beginner Decision Framework

1. COST FUNCTIONS

\[ C(x) = F + vx \]
Fixed Cost (\(F\)): Rent, bills (constant even at 0 units).
Variable Cost (\(v\)): Materials, packaging (\(v \times x\)).
Nonlinear: \(C(x) = ax^2 + bx + F\) (overtime, extra wear).

2. REVENUE FUNCTIONS

\[ R(x) = px \]
Revenue: Selling Price \(\times\) Quantity Sold.
Changing Price: \(p(x) = 100 - 2x\) • find price first, then multiply by quantity.
Higher volume can lower unit price!

3. PROFIT & BREAK-EVEN

\[ P(x) = R(x) - C(x) \] \[ Q = \frac{F}{p - v} \]
Break-Even: Revenue = Cost \(\implies\) Profit = 0.
Contribution: \((p - v)\) covers fixed costs first.
\(x < Q \to\) Loss • \(x > Q \to\) Profit

4. MARKET EQUILIBRIUM

\[ Q_d = Q_s \]
Demand (\(Q_d = a - bp\)): Customers buy less when price rises.
Supply (\(Q_s = c + dp\)): Sellers supply more when price rises.
Equilibrium: Set \(Q_d = Q_s\), solve price \(p\), then substitute to get \(Q\).

5. EVALUATING LIMITS

\[ \lim_{x\to a} f(x) \]
Concept: What number does the output get closer to?
Direct Substitution: Replace \(x\) with the approaching number.
If \(\frac{0}{0}\) (Indeterminate): Factor numerator & cancel before substituting!

6. CONTINUITY CHECKLIST

\[ \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a) \]
1. \(f(a)\) exists (defined)
2. Limit exists (Left = Right)
3. Limit = Value (seamless)
Discontinuous when fees jump at distance/volume tiers.
